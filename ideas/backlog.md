### P1. Agent-file Linter: drift and rot checker for AGENTS.md / CLAUDE.md / skills [proposed]
**Concept:** A CLI plus GitHub Action that checks agent instruction files against the real repo (referenced paths, scripts, packages exist), flags AGENTS.md vs CLAUDE.md divergence, bloat and contradictory rules.
- **Trend evidence:** Many posts describe "rules-file rot" and say no tool validates these files against reality: [Getunblocked, Rules-File Rot](https://getunblocked.com/blog/rules-file-rot), [Thoughtworks Radar, agent instruction bloat](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-sg/radar/techniques/agent-instruction-bloat), [Addy Osmani, Audit your Agent files](https://addyosmani.com/blog/audit-your-agent-files/), [DEV, "Your CLAUDE.md is a lie"](https://dev.to/mjkloski/your-claudemd-is-a-lie-2h9j). An arXiv study ([2606.09090](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09090)) reports AGENTS.md at about 43% and CLAUDE.md at about 30% of agent config files. Post dates were not visible in search results.
- **Who / pain:** Teams and solo devs using coding agents; stale instructions are trusted blindly and fail silently.
- **Why now:** Agent config files are now mainstream and growing; the "audit" advice exists but the tooling is manual.
- **Our angle:** We live this problem (this repo has CLAUDE.md, skills, agents, flows). Fully buildable and demoable as software, dogfood-able on our own repo, no spend or deploy needed to demo.
- **Effort:** M. **Main risk:** Thin moat; a big vendor (or a Claude Code/GitHub feature) could absorb it, and some free scripts may already exist (not checked).
- **Confidence:** Medium-high on the need; unverified: existing competing tools, actual willingness to pay or install.

### P2. Local-first agent spend lens: per-task token cost attribution from agent logs [approved]
**Concept:** Reads local coding-agent session logs and shows cost by task/skill/subagent, detects runaway or wasteful sessions, with no cloud account needed.
- **Trend evidence:** Complaints that token counts appear only at session end and are opaque ([WebProNews on Claude Code billing, undated](https://www.webpronews.com/claude-codes-hidden-cost-problem-developers-sound-the-alarm-over-anthropics-opaque-token-billing/)); bills rising despite lower token prices ([Elastic](https://www.elastic.co/blog/token-costs-ai-bills)); [LangChain, fix your coding agent bill](https://langchain.com/blog/fix-your-coding-agent-bill); [1Password, AI spend hangover](https://1password.com/blog/how-to-survive-the-ai-spend-hangover); [Kilo Code on spending caps](https://dev.to/kilocode/stop-looking-for-ai-coding-spending-caps-42d9). Enterprise vendors (Datadog, IBM, WorkOS) are also publishing on this.
- **Who / pain:** Individual devs and small teams who cannot see why a session was expensive.
- **Why now:** Agentic usage makes spend unpredictable; the discourse is loud.
- **Our angle:** Local, private, per-skill attribution fits our skill/subagent structure; demoable on our own run logs (`runs/`).
- **Effort:** M. **Main risk:** Crowded space with funded players; log formats vary by tool and may change.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Mostly vendor/blog sources, dates unclear; could not verify demand for a free local tool vs built-in vendor dashboards.

### P3. MCP config auditor: permission and trust scanner for local MCP setups [proposed]
**Concept:** Scans a user's MCP client configs and servers for over-broad scopes, unauthenticated or exposed endpoints, risky tool descriptions (poisoning patterns) and the "lethal trifecta" combination, then outputs a least-privilege report.
- **Trend evidence:** [Microsoft Security, State of MCP security in 2026](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft-security-blog/the-state-of-mcp-security-in-2026/4531327); [CSA, 7 MCP risks for CISOs (2026-06-15)](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2026/06/15/7-mcp-risks-cisos-should-consider-and-how-to-prepare); CSA guide cites about 7,000 exposed MCP servers, roughly half unauthenticated, and 30+ CVEs in Jan-Feb 2026 (secondary citation, not verified at source).
- **Who / pain:** Developers and small security teams wiring many MCP servers with no review step.
- **Why now:** MCP adoption is wide and the security literature is converging on concrete checks.
- **Our angle:** Static analysis of config files is feasible without network access; clear checklist from public guidance.
- **Effort:** M. **Main risk:** Security tooling credibility; false confidence; fast-moving threat landscape and incumbents (gateway vendors).
- **Confidence:** Medium. Risk is well documented; unverified: whether individuals/SMBs want a scanner vs enterprise gateways.

- **Confidence:** Low-medium; fine claims and vendor stats unverified.

### G1. Slime Strip: an idle loot ARPG that lives on a strip of your desktop [proposed]
**Concept:** A slime hero auto-fights along a thin always-on-top strip above the taskbar while you work; loot drops with procedural affixes, and a small, rewarding active layer (equip, craft, prestige) happens when you glance down.
- **Trend evidence:** TBH peak about 526k-546k concurrent in June 2026; Deskrawl over 43k concurrent in Oct 2026; Desktop Defender Very Positive at $4.99; Desktop Companion tag official May 2026 (links in shared notes). Hybrid is clearly where demand is, but also where 1,566 idlers have launched this year.
- **Who / what they want:** Players with a second or idle screen region who want a loot dopamine loop without attention cost; they punish CPU drain, oversized strips, and broken economies.
- **Why now:** Official tag, Next Fest and Idler Fest visibility, and proven demand within the last 6 months.
- **How we leverage it:** Differentiate on what reviews complain about: tiny CPU budget (sleep when occluded or fullscreen app active), resizable and truly transparent strip, multi-monitor safe, no Steam Market economy, offline progress, local-only saves. Slime hero plus an "office" skin (each loot slot is an AI agent role) as flavour. Loot is pure data and math, which agents build and test well.
- **Engine / art:** Godot 4.x (small, free, MIT, scripts easy for agents; GodotSteam later) with code-drawn art: slimes as procedural blobs (shader or `_draw()` with squash and stretch), enemies as geometric sprites generated at startup, item icons composed from shapes and palette. No external assets needed.
- **Rough effort:** M. **First playable in a few days:** a transparent borderless strip window; slime auto-walks and fights 3 enemy types; loot drops with 4 rarities and random affixes; inventory/equip popup on click; offline-progress calculation; save file; CPU idle under a target when the window is hidden.
- **Main risk:** Most crowded sub-niche (idle flood, near-identical clones), and the top titles lean on trading economies we are deliberately avoiding. Also Godot window pitfalls above (focus loss, passthrough bugs).
- **Confidence:** Medium. Unverified: engines and team sizes of TBH/Deskrawl, true sales of any title, long-term retention (no retention data found), Steam overlay behaviour with our window.

### G2. Slime Office: cozy desktop idle sim where a slime team "works" in a tiny office on your screen [proposed]
**Concept:** A tiny office cross-section sits above the taskbar; slime workers produce coins/ideas, upgrade desks and gadgets, and the player decorates; cozy loop in the Rusty's Retirement / Tiny Pasture lineage.
- **Trend evidence:** Rusty's Retirement about 200k sales and 96-97% positive; Tiny Pasture 5,640 concurrent at launch and about 7k Very Positive reviews at $5.99; PC Gamer: "most surprising cozy trend this year was the desktop pet renaissance" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/the-most-surprising-cozy-game-trend-this-year-was-the-desktop-pet-renaissance), snippet only). Tiny Terraces creator reported about $26.8k gross in its first month on 5,000+ early-access copies (single-creator claim, [GamePressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/how-much-can-you-earn-as-an-indie-dev-tiny-terraces-creator-share/zb889b), unverified).
- **Who / what they want:** Cozy, low-stress players who want a calm desk-side companion and gentle progress; they punish performance drain and shallow endings.
- **Why now:** Cozy plus desktop is the best-reviewed corner of the niche (97% for Rusty's), and the new tag gives it a shelf.
- **How we leverage it:** Our own "office of agents" is literally the theme; a pixel-office with slime workers is charming and fits our sprite/office-view work. Layered code-drawn rooms, pixel-slime sprites generated by code at startup.
- **Engine / art:** Godot 4.x (2D, UI-heavy) or Unity if we want the proven Rusty's path; code-drawn tilemap rooms and procedural slime blobs with accessory overlays (hats, glasses) drawn from shapes.
- **Rough effort:** M. **First playable in a few days:** strip window with a 3-room office, 3 slime workers wandering with simple state machines, a coin economy with 10 upgrades, decorate mode with 6 items, save and offline gains.
- **Main risk:** Cozy-idle is also crowded, and cozy audiences review on charm, which procedural art may under-deliver; retention beyond the first hours unproven.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Unverified: Rusty's player-hours and retention, Tiny Terraces numbers, whether code-drawn art is accepted by this audience.

### G3. Slime Trail: an idle expedition adventure with occasional choices [proposed]
**Concept:** A slime explorer walks a long procedurally generated trail along the taskbar; every few minutes an event card (find, fight, NPC, branching choice) pops up in a small bubble, and the story persists across days. Adventure tag with desktop-companion pacing.
- **Trend evidence:** Weyrdlets (pet digging for loot on desktop and island, Mostly Positive, 272 reviews, release July 2024, price conflicting across sources); Deskape (base-building survival in a quarter-screen strip, PC Gamer); Next Fest idle demos with narrative hooks. Evidence for adventure-flavoured desktop games is thin: I found no breakout, only small to mid titles. Reviews of The Longing style "wait-for-real-time" games exist (Idler Fest lineup, [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Idler-Steam-Fest-starts-now-and-lasts-till-Feb-10.956428.0.html)).
- **Who / what they want:** Players who like story and discovery but cannot give focused time; they want content depth, not just numbers.
- **Why now:** Idler market is number-heavy and visually samey; a narrative idler is a gap, though not a proven demand pocket.
- **How we leverage it:** Event text and branching content is cheap for AI agents to generate and test in bulk (authored as data files, not live AI, so no live-generation disclosure issue; pre-generated text may still need disclosure if we use AI-written text: check at release). Slime and anime flavour via a tiny cast.
- **Engine / art:** Godot 4.x (Dialogue/Resource data, good text rendering); code-drawn parallax trail, procedural slime and landmarks.
- **Rough effort:** M. **First playable in a few days:** strip window, auto-walking slime, 40 event cards with 2-3 choices, three biomes, persistent run state and offline travel, a recap panel.
- **Main risk:** Content volume is the whole game; writing quality of generated text can feel like slop (players are hostile to it), and demand is unproven.
- **Confidence:** Low-medium. No quantified adventure-idler hit found; engine and scale of Weyrdlets unverified.

### G4. Slime Pal: a free-to-start desktop pet with light idle digging [proposed]
**Concept:** A customizable slime that roams, sleeps, reacts to cursor and time of day, and digs up small collectibles while idle; free base with cosmetic add-ons.
- **Trend evidence:** Pocket Waifu: Desktop Pet (free, Very Positive, about 1.5k-1.7k reviews, [Dekudeals](https://www.dekudeals.com/items/pocket-waifu-desktop-pet)); Desktop Mate (free, about 9k reviews but Mixed, about 606k estimated copies by third-party model); Weyrdlets blends pet and idle loot; Desktop Companion now an official tag. Many small copycats (Desktop Purrfect 24 reviews, Collie Companion 3 reviews, Desktop Pet: Idle Cats none) show the long tail is thin.
- **Who / what they want:** Anime and cozy fans wanting a pet or mascot without work; they complain about performance and, for Desktop Mate, controversy and DLC friction (anecdotal).
- **Why now:** Official tag, steady interest, and a very small first build.
- **How we leverage it:** Fastest to ship; slime anatomy (soft body, squash, blob merge) is easy to code-draw, and it is the safest first demo of the Godot window stack that G1-G3 would reuse. Anime flavour optional through expression sets.
- **Engine / art:** Godot 4.x; soft-body slime via shader or Line2D metaballs, expressions drawn in code.
- **Rough effort:** S. **First playable in a few days:** transparent window, click-through except on the slime, drag-and-drop, 5 moods, eat/sleep/wander, and 10 digging finds with an idle timer.
- **Main risk:** Low monetization ceiling (free or about $5) and a very crowded long tail; companion apps struggle to retain players beyond novelty. Free price means revenue depends on cosmetic DLC.
- **Confidence:** Medium on demand for free pets, low on revenue. Desktop Mate numbers are model estimates.

### G5. Slimeloot Survivors: a 15-minute ARPG-lite / bullet-heaven run with loot-driven builds [proposed]
**Concept:** Top-down auto-attack slime roguelite where drops reshape the build; short runs, meta-unlocks, one boss.
- **Trend evidence:** Megabonk (Unity, solo, $10, 1M copies in two weeks, 102k peak concurrent, Sept 2025: [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/roguelike/out-of-nowhere-roguelike-hit-megabonk-has-sold-1-million-copies-in-2-weeks-solo-creator-says-ill-be-eating-spaghetti-with-extra-sauce-tonight)); Dwarves: Glory, Death and Loot reported about 250k-300k copies; "Bullet Heaven" became an official tag in May 2026.
- **Who / what they want:** Mainstream PC roguelite players; they want novelty, juice and build depth.
- **Why now:** Tag is official and the audience is large, but I would rate this the most saturated and hit-driven of the five; outliers like Megabonk are survivorship bias.
- **How we leverage it:** Builds and loot are pure data and systems that agents can balance-test. Slime theme is natural (merging and splitting).
- **Engine / art:** Godot 4.x (Slay the Spire 2's engine is reported to be Godot, per [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/roguelike/steam-stats-for-slay-the-spire-2s-engine-godot-show-strong-signs-of-exponential-growth/), snippet) with code-drawn blobs and particle-heavy effects.
- **Rough effort:** L to be competitive; M for a vertical slice. **First playable in a few days:** arena, auto-attack slime, 3 enemy waves, XP gems, 12 loot items with stacking affixes, run-end screen.
- **Main risk:** Needs polish and "juice" that code-drawn art and a no-human team find hard; most releases in this tag earn almost nothing, and it is no fit for the "lives on your desktop" angle.
- **Confidence:** Low-medium. Evidence is outlier hits; no data on median outcomes for the tag.
